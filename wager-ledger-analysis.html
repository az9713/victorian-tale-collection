The Wager Ledger, Books 1 and 2: analysis and rubric

This page scores The Snuffbox (1881) and The Cable (1887) on 28 dimensions and lists the debts as dimension 29. The purpose is to make Books 3 to 5 richer and more entertaining than Books 1 and 2. Date of the analysis: 2026-11-23.

Overview

The two books are sound and well made. The two books are less strong as entertainment. The entertainment layer is where Books 3 to 5 can get the largest increase.

Main findings

  1. Soundness and craft are near the maximum. The two layers together give 39 of 45 for the Snuffbox and 35 of 45 for the Cable.
  2. Entertainment is lower. The layer gives 43 of 70 for the Snuffbox and 40 of 70 for the Cable.
  3. Two dimensions are low in both books. Deadline gets 1 and 1. Opponent gets 2 and 1. No case has a time limit. No opponent speaks one word.
  4. The Cable has more charm. The Snuffbox has more engine. The Cable is higher on 4 entertainment dimensions (world texture, secondary cast, layering, crack in a lead). The Snuffbox is higher on 8.
  5. The Cable has four logic defects. The largest is the set of Gerrard Street dates. Open dimension A1 for the dates.
  6. The pictures are well painted and not exact. Picture craft gets 4 and 4. Picture-to-text fidelity gets 2 and 3. Each book has only 2 pictures.

Rubric and scores

The rubric has five layers. I give no average across the layers, because the layers measure different objects. Each defect lowers one dimension only. Select a row to see the definition, the evidence with line references, and the instruction for the next books.

Defect scale (Soundness, Craft, Series, and three Package dimensions)
  • 5: I found no defect.
  • 4: one small defect. Most readers will not see the defect.
  • 3: a defect that an attentive reader sees.
  • 2: a defect that weakens the main effect of the story.
  • 1: the story fails on this dimension.
Reach scale (Entertainment, and picture coverage). This scale is new. A story with no defect can get 1 on this scale.
  • 5: a reader will tell a different person about this part.
  • 4: strong. The part does its work in more than one scene.
  • 3: present. The part does its work one time.
  • 2: thin. The part is there, but the reader gets little from the part.
  • 1: absent.

Continuity debts (dimension D2)

A debt is not a defect of Books 1 and 2. A debt is a promise or a gap that a later book must pay.

  1. Aouda. Verne's book ends with the marriage. The house has no mistress in 1881 or in 1887 (01-the-snuffbox-1881-v3.md:65-71, 02-the-cable-1887.md:148). The plan names "an ageing Aouda as Fogg's one crack". A later book must explain the absence.
  2. Fix. The plan names him as "a bitter, failed would-be detective". He is not yet in a book.
  3. "Why?" (02-the-cable-1887.md:164). A later book must answer the question, or refuse the answer on purpose.
  4. Rule 4. The cost of a life lived as an equation must arrive "in a late scene without anyone saying it".
  5. Ages. In 1894 Fogg is 62 and Holmes is 40. In 1909 Fogg is 77 and Holmes is 55, if Holmes is 27 in 1881 (01-the-snuffbox-1881-v3.md:7).
  6. The steward's age. By Verne he is 52 in 1894 and 67 in 1909. By Book 1 he is approximately 63 and 78. One of the two numbers must become the rule.
  7. The ledger and the house. "The ledger does not leave the house" (02-the-cable-1887.md:92) conflicts with the ledger at the club (:43, :69). Later books must select one rule.
  8. Venn and the club. Venn is the client in both books (01-the-snuffbox-1881-v3.md:5, 02-the-cable-1887.md:19). Both meetings are at the Reform Club. A third use makes a fixed device.
  9. The ending. Both books end with a written note. Five books with the same ending give a pattern that the reader can predict.
  10. The doctor and the annual. The manuscript is "with a publisher of Christmas annuals" (02-the-cable-1887.md:25). Fogg does "not entirely see why" Holmes is famous (:156). A later book can use Fogg as a reader.

Not checked, from memory: in Doyle, Holmes is thought dead from 1891 to 1894, and Holmes retires to Sussex before 1909. Check the two facts before the briefs for 1894 and 1909.

Levers for richer, more entertaining stories

Each lever comes from a low score. The order is from the largest gap to the smallest gap. The "how" lines are proposals, not decisions.

What to keep

  • A third party or a case causes each meeting (B1: 5 and 5).
  • One physical action shows a character (B2). Example: nine tricks played to the end (01-the-snuffbox-1881-v3.md:39).
  • The humour comes from exact statements (B3). Example: "declined to illuminate, on the grounds that it would be visible" (02-the-cable-1887.md:148).
  • The backdrop causes events (C11). Example: the Jubilee stands change the walk (02-the-cable-1887.md:9).
  • A written record gives the proof (A1). Example: the ledger entry (02-the-cable-1887.md:71-72).
  • Each man keeps a written line about the other man (D1).
